# Name study (November 2026)

Should Ember Vault keep its name now that it's going multi-system, and which domain should we buy?

**Short answer: rename.** "Embervault" is already used by a co-op game-save sync tool (embervault.app), by *Roots of Embervault* (a crypto pixel RPG published by YGG Play), and by a "local-first notes" app on GitHub. Its .com, .io, .net and .gg are registered by other people. No trademark blocks it, but in search results we'd share a name with two gaming products and a notes app. The listing is about 10 days old, so a rename now costs almost nothing. Later it costs real users.

**Recommendation after both rounds: Myriadlore** (myriadlore.com). Of every name checked, it says what the product is most directly: *a myriad of worlds, and their lore*. That's multi-system plus campaign notes, the way "LegendKeeper" and "World Anvil" explain themselves. If you'd rather have a cool word that keeps the fire look, take **Moltenlore**. If you want the plainest name, take **Campaignstead**. The full combined ranking is in [Both rounds, ranked](#both-rounds-ranked).

Two rounds:
- **Round 1:** fire and vault names, 1,408 checked.
- **Round 2:** any name that says "multi-system campaign notes", about 2,100 checked. This round covered plain-meaning compounds (LegendKeeper / World Anvil style), multi-system words (myriad, omni, poly, many), GM-role words (keeper, warden, narrator, referee), TTRPG phrases (session zero, long rest, plot hook), and rare or coined single words (orrery, gazetteer, bindery, lorery).

## Round 1: how it was run

1. **Criteria.** The name has to:
   - work for any game system;
   - avoid "Obsidian", "D&D", "Nimble" and other trademarks;
   - be easy to say and spell after hearing it once;
   - fit the fire look we already have (orange d20, floating embers, lava crits), so the logo and site survive;
   - make its .com available. A name with an open .com ranked higher.
2. **Comparable names.** The space names itself evocatively: Roll20, Foundry, Owlbear Rodeo, Fantasy Grounds, Talespire, World Anvil, LegendKeeper, Kanka, Saga20, Demiplane. "Keeper", "Anvil", "Forge" and "Saga" are already taken by competitors.
3. **Generation.** 1,408 names: fire words (ember, cinder, flint, torch, wick, pyre, molten, magma…) × story, table and vault words (lore, tale, tome, hearth, moot, keep, hold…), plus hand-made names.
4. **.com check.** Every name went through Verisign's registry lookup (RDAP), which says authoritatively whether a .com is registered. **321 were unregistered.** Nearly every "Ember…" and "…Lore" .com is gone, and so is every single dictionary word.
5. **Finalists.** I picked 20 by hand from those 321, plus Lorehearth and Ember Vault as baselines. For each, I checked .app (Google registry RDAP) and .gg, .io, .games and .net (DNS). **All six were open for all 20 finalists.**
6. **Clash research.** For each finalist, three research agents searched the web, Steam, itch.io and GitHub for existing products and searched trademark mirrors (Justia, Markinton, Gleanmark). I also searched the Obsidian plugin directory (10,490 plugins) myself.

Limits:
- **Trademarks:** this is not a formal USPTO clearance. The mirrors only show what web search turns up.
- **Pricing:** RDAP says a domain is unregistered, not that it's sold at the normal price. Check the price at the registrar.
- **Handles:** X, Reddit and Steam handles weren't checked (they need a login). itch.io subdomains were free for the names checked.

## Round 1: why Moltenlore led

- **It ties the plugin to Obsidian without using the word.** Obsidian is volcanic glass, formed when molten lava cools fast. "Molten lore" is your campaign in the forge before it sets into your vault.
- **The brand carries over.** The orange gradient, the embers and the lava crits all already say "molten". The d20 logo stays.
- **It works when spoken.** It passes the "tell a friend at the table" test: no silent letters, no doubled letters at the join, and only one obvious spelling.
- **It's multi-system.** Nothing in it says Nimble or 5e.
- **It's clean.** No product, game, GitHub user or trademark uses it, and moltenlore.com plus the other five domains checked are open.

**Runetorch** is the safer, plainer choice if "Moltenlore" feels too long. **Embermoot** keeps the most of what exists today and costs the least to rebrand, but it carries the weakest word.

## Round 2: names that say what it is

**What the name should say.** The product is a GM's campaign notebook that lives in your notes and works for any game system. The names that do this well explain themselves in two words: LegendKeeper keeps legends, World Anvil forges worlds. The other route is a cool word that becomes a brand (Foundry, Alchemy), which costs a tagline.

**What the domain search showed:**
- **Every single word is gone as a .com:** Orrery, Gazetteer, Bindery, Astrolabe, Polyhedral, Omnibus, Skald, Lodestar, Sidequest, Session Zero, Plothook, Tablekeeper, Myriad, Omen.
- **Every obvious compound is gone too:** Campaignkeeper, Lorebook, Questbook, Storyloom, Taleweaver, Partyledger.
- **What's left** is uncommon pairings (Myriad…, Omen…, Session…) and coined forms.

- **Myriad** means many worlds, which is the multi-system part.

## Round 3: "…keeper" names

**Why keeper.** It works the way LegendKeeper and Pathfinder do: take the word apart and it explains itself. The catch is that **"-keeper" is already crowded in exactly this niche**:
- **LegendKeeper:** a worldbuilding and campaign wiki.
- **LoreKeeper** (lorekeeper.com): an AI D&D campaign-prep assistant.
- **SessionKeeper:** a D&D notes app on iOS.
- **Campaign Keeper:** a TTRPG web app.
- **Realm Keeper:** a worldbuilding wiki with maps.
- **Table Keeper:** a free D&D DM-screen app.
- Keeper is also the GM's title in *Call of Cthulhu*.

Every obvious first word is owned by a competitor. A keeper name only stands apart if its first word is distinctive.

**Domains.** I checked 240 keeper names. 54 have both the .com and the .app open. Lorekeeper, Talekeeper, Questkeeper, Storykeeper, Campaignkeeper, Sessionkeeper, Realmkeeper, Worldkeeper, Tomekeeper and Sagakeeper .coms are all registered.

**Pick from this round: Cinderkeeper.** It's the only keeper name that is clear of clashes, avoids sounding like LegendKeeper, has every domain open, and keeps today's brand. The meaning needs one line of tagline ("Keep your campaign's fire burning"), but once heard it sticks, the way Pathfinder does.

**Keeping Ember Vault on embervault.dev** is possible (.dev is open, about $12 a year). But people type .com and .app by habit, and embervault.app is a game-save tool, so some of your visitors would go there. You'd also be competing for the plain name in search. That's fine as a stopgap, but weak long term.

**Molten Vault:** moltenvault.com has been registered since 2019 with no site. The .app, .dev, .gg, .io and .net are open. Not clash-researched.

## Round 4: shard riffs

**The idea.** Obsidian is knapped into shards, and a campaign splits into shards and facets: sessions, NPCs, game systems.

**Shardkeeper** has a minor clash:
- **Domains:** the .com is taken (see below). The .app, .dev, .gg, .io, .net and .games are open, and so are **shardkeepers.com**, **theshardkeeper.com** and **getshardkeeper.com**. The GitHub handle is free.
- **No trademarks found.** That includes Sanderson's Cosmere: "Shardkeeper" isn't one of its terms. The Cosmere's real words are Shardblade, Shardbearer and Shardholder.
- **shardkeeper.com:** an early prototype of a 2D kids' platformer by a solo developer (registered March 2026). It has no Steam page or press yet. If it ships, it competes with you for the exact name in search.
- **Other games with the name:** "Shardkeeper: Mage Survivors", an itch.io demo in development, and "Shardkeep", a "coming soon" Steam deckbuilder. World of Warcraft also has a "Twilight Shardkeeper" NPC.
- **The real risk is Shard Tabletop:** an active indie D&D 5e virtual tabletop with character sheets, a campaign journal and dice. Its Discord has about 1,800 members. It's a near-identical feature list with "Shard" as the stressed first syllable, so a GM who knows Shard may read "Shardkeeper" as a Shard add-on. The Obsidian-plugin context softens this. Shard Studios also holds a SHARD RPG trademark for TTRPG products, though not for software.

**The riffs** (.com, .app, .dev, .gg, .io, .net and .games open for all):

|---|---|---|
| **Flintkeeper** | Clear | Flint is knapped like obsidian, and flint strikes sparks, which ties to the ember look. The cleanest name in this round. Search noise from fire-starter products. |
| Facetkeeper | Clear | Weak RPG flavour; heard as "faucet". |
| Shardwarden | Minor clash | Shard Tabletop overlap. |
| Shardscribe | Minor clash | Reads like a Shard Tabletop add-on. |

## Before you buy

1. Say your top two out loud to someone who hasn't seen them, then ask them to type it.
2. USPTO search (tmsearch.uspto.gov) for the exact name in Classes 9 and 42 (software) and 28 and 41 (games, entertainment). If it's clear, buy the .com. Optionally also buy the .app and .gg so nobody squats them. Each costs about $10–20 a year.
3. Grab the handles you'll use: itch.io, X, Reddit, a Discord server name.
4. Then follow `prototypes/RENAME.md` with the new name: domain first, then the display name, description and repo. The plugin id stays `nimble-gm`.
